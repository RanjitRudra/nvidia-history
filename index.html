Chapter 01
The Denny's Bet
How a meeting over eggs and coffee gave birth to the company that would power the AI revolution.
"We also observed that video games were simultaneously one of the most computationally challenging problems and the largest market opportunity in computing." — Jensen Huang, on why NVIDIA was founded
📅
April 5, 1993
NVIDIA is officially incorporated. The name comes from "invidia" — Latin for envy — and from the internal file prefix "NV" used by the founders, standing for "next version."
🍳
The Denny's Meeting
In late 1992, Jensen Huang, Chris Malachowsky, and Curtis Priem met at a Denny's on Berryessa Road in East San Jose. They agreed to start a company around one big idea: dedicated graphics chips would unlock computing challenges CPUs couldn't solve.
💰
$40,000 Seed
The trio pooled personal savings of around $40,000 to start. Within the first year they secured ~$20 million in venture capital from Sequoia Capital and Sutter Hill Ventures — one of the best VC bets in history.
🏭
Fabless from Day One
NVIDIA was founded as a fabless semiconductor company — it designs chips but outsources manufacturing to foundries like TSMC. This model allowed rapid innovation without the capital costs of building fabs.
🎯
The Core Vision
The founders identified that CPUs were terrible at parallel tasks like 3D graphics. Specialized parallel processors — GPUs — would be orders of magnitude better. This insight would eventually power all of modern AI.
🌍
The Market in 1993
When NVIDIA launched, roughly 90 graphics chip companies competed in the same space — S3, ATI, Matrox, Trident, Cirrus Logic. Only one would dominate. The odds were terrible. Jensen bet anyway.
Chapter 02
The Founders
Three engineers from three companies with one shared vision.
JH
Jensen Huang
Co-founder, President & CEO — 1993 to Present
Taiwanese-American electrical engineer born in Tainan, Taiwan. Came to the US at age 9. Worked as a dishwasher at Denny's as a teenager in Portland — the same chain where NVIDIA was conceptualized. Studied at Oregon State University and Stanford (MS EE). Previously Director of CoreWare at LSI Logic and microprocessor designer at AMD. Has led NVIDIA as CEO for over 32 years — one of the longest-tenured CEOs in Silicon Valley history. Known for his leather jacket, direct style, and singular long-term vision. Called "the most important person in tech" by many analysts during the AI boom.
CM
Chris Malachowsky
Co-founder, Engineer
Previously an engineer at Sun Microsystems where he was frustrated with management direction. He and Curtis Priem were actively looking to leave Sun when Jensen Huang convinced them to start something new. Malachowsky brought deep hardware engineering expertise and Sun's system-level thinking to NVIDIA's early chip designs.
CP
Curtis Priem
Co-founder, Chief Technologist
Senior staff engineer and graphics chip designer at both IBM and Sun Microsystems. Priem was the deepest graphics architecture expert of the three and contributed the foundational chip design philosophy. He later donated $50 million to Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute (RPI) and eventually transitioned away from day-to-day operations as NVIDIA scaled.
Chapter 03
The Timeline
Every major milestone from 1993 to 2026.
1993
NVIDIA Founded
April 5 — Jensen Huang, Chris Malachowsky, and Curtis Priem incorporate NVIDIA in Santa Clara, CA. First partner: SGS-Thomson (now STMicroelectronics).
1995
NV1 — First Product (Commercial Failure)
NVIDIA's first GPU, the NV1, launches — a multi-function PCI card combining 2D/3D graphics, audio, and game ports. Its quadratic texture mapping approach conflicts with Microsoft's DirectX triangle-based standard. NVIDIA nearly goes bankrupt. Jensen later calls this period "the two worst years of my professional life."
1997
RIVA 128 — The Comeback
NVIDIA pivots hard to DirectX compliance and releases the RIVA 128 — a 128-bit 3D processor that sells over 1 million units in its first four months. The company survives and gains real momentum. Partnerships with Dell, Gateway, and Micron follow.
🚀 MAJOR MILESTONE
1999
GeForce 256 — The GPU is Born + IPO
NVIDIA coins the term "GPU" and launches the GeForce 256 — the world's first chip to perform hardware transform and lighting, offloading this from the CPU for the first time. NVIDIA goes public on NASDAQ at $12/share on January 22. This redefines PC architecture and launches the modern gaming industry.
2000
3dfx Acquisition
NVIDIA acquires the assets of 3dfx Interactive — its biggest gaming rival and maker of the famous Voodoo cards — for $70 million. This removes NVIDIA's main competitor and consolidates the discrete GPU market.
2001
Xbox Graphics Deal
NVIDIA wins the contract to provide graphics for Microsoft's original Xbox game console — a major validation of the GeForce architecture and a huge revenue driver.
2003
Quadro & Tesla — Professional & Compute
NVIDIA expands beyond gaming into professional visualization (Quadro line for CAD/design workstations) and begins early work on GPU computing for scientific applications. Revenue approaches $1B.
🔑 DEFINING MOMENT
2006
CUDA Launched — The Bet That Changed Everything
NVIDIA launches CUDA (Compute Unified Device Architecture) — a parallel computing platform that opens GPUs to general-purpose computation beyond graphics. Scientists, researchers, and eventually AI developers can now write code that runs on NVIDIA GPUs. This single decision seeds the entire AI revolution. At the time, investors were skeptical. Jensen was not.
2008
Tesla GPU Computing Line
NVIDIA launches the Tesla product line — GPUs purpose-built for HPC and scientific computing. These become the foundation for data center GPU computing. Revenue hits $3.5B.
2011
Tegra Mobile & Automotive
NVIDIA introduces its Tegra line of mobile/embedded processors. The company begins exploring automotive computing — a strategic move that would eventually lead to the DRIVE platform for autonomous vehicles.
⚡ AI IGNITION
2012
AlexNet Changes History
AlexNet, a deep neural network trained on two NVIDIA GTX 580 GPUs by researchers at University of Toronto, wins the ImageNet competition by a massive margin over CPU-based approaches. This moment — dubbed "First Contact" — proves that GPUs + deep learning = the future of AI. Every major AI lab immediately begins buying NVIDIA GPUs.
2016
Pascal Architecture + DGX-1 Supercomputer
NVIDIA launches the Pascal architecture (P100) and the DGX-1 — the world's first AI supercomputer in a box. Jensen personally delivers the first DGX-1 to OpenAI. Revenue reaches $6.9B. The data center business begins its ascent.
2017
Volta + Toyota Partnership
Volta architecture launches with Tensor Cores — specialized hardware for AI matrix math that delivers massive deep learning speedups. Toyota partners with NVIDIA for autonomous vehicle AI using the Drive PX platform. NVIDIA reaches $9.7B revenue.
2018
RTX & Real-Time Ray Tracing
NVIDIA launches the Turing architecture with RTX — the first GPU capable of real-time ray tracing, a fundamental shift in how light and shadow are rendered in games and professional visualization. The RTX brand becomes synonymous with premium graphics.
🏆 BIG ACQUISITION
2019–2020
Mellanox Acquisition — $6.9 Billion
NVIDIA acquires Mellanox Technologies — the leading maker of InfiniBand and high-speed networking interconnects used in data centers and HPC clusters — for $6.9B. This gives NVIDIA end-to-end control of the AI data center stack: GPUs + networking. The Mellanox team is the backbone of today's NVLink and NVSwitch technologies.
2020
Ampere Architecture
The A100 GPU launches — purpose-built for AI. It becomes the workhorse of data center AI for three years. NVIDIA attempts to acquire Arm Holdings for $40B — the deal is ultimately blocked by regulators in 2022 on antitrust grounds. Revenue: $16.7B.
2022
Hopper Architecture — The H100
The H100 GPU launches, featuring fourth-generation Tensor Cores and the Transformer Engine — purpose-built for the large language model era. This is the GPU that trains ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and every major AI model. Demand immediately outstrips supply.
💎 $1 TRILLION
2023
NVIDIA Crosses $1 Trillion Valuation
June 2023 — NVIDIA becomes only the fifth US company to reach a $1 trillion market cap, joining Apple, Microsoft, Alphabet, and Amazon. Q2 revenue guidance of $11B shocks Wall Street (consensus was $7B). The AI boom is now undeniable. Revenue for the full year: $44B.
🚀 BLACKWELL ERA
2024
Blackwell Architecture + $2T → $3T → $4T
NVIDIA crosses $2T in February, $3T in June, and $4T in October. The Blackwell architecture launches — 208 billion transistors, 30x faster than Hopper for AI inference. Jensen Huang announces Vera Rubin as the next generation. Revenue: $130.5B. NVIDIA is now the most valuable company in the world for stretches of time.
🌟 TODAY
2025–2026
$5 Trillion + Vera Rubin + $216B Revenue
NVIDIA becomes the world's first $5 trillion company in October 2025. Full-year FY2026 revenue hits $216B — up 65% year-over-year. Data center revenue reaches $197B. Q1 FY2027 revenue: $81.6B (85% YoY growth). Vera Rubin architecture enters production — 3.5x faster than Blackwell. Jensen projects $1 trillion in chip orders through 2027.

Chapter 07
Today & Future
Where NVIDIA stands in 2026 and where it's headed next.
"The buildout of AI factories — the largest infrastructure expansion in human history — is accelerating." — Jensen Huang, May 2026 Earnings Call
🤖
Agentic AI Era
Reasoning AI models like GPT-5 and Claude 4 consume up to 1,000x more compute than earlier models because they "think" before responding. Jensen says this makes demand "essentially infinite" for GPU compute.
⚡
Vera Rubin (2026)
3.5x faster than Blackwell on training, 5x faster on inference. 50 petaFLOPs of FP4 per chip. HBM4 memory. The Vera Rubin rack system has 1.3 million components including 72 Rubin GPUs and 36 Vera CPUs. In production now.
🌐
Sovereign AI
Governments and countries are building national AI infrastructure — buying NVIDIA systems to power their own AI capabilities. This is a new and rapidly growing market segment Jensen calls "Sovereign AI."
🚗
Automotive Explosion
NVIDIA's automotive segment grew 32% YoY in FY2026. The DRIVE platform powers hundreds of vehicles from Toyota, Mercedes, BYD, and others. Jensen calls it NVIDIA's "next billion-dollar business."
🏭
Physical AI & Robotics
Isaac and Omniverse platforms are the foundation for physical AI — robots and automated systems that understand and navigate the physical world. NVIDIA sees this as the next wave after language AI.
🔬
Regulatory Headwinds
NVIDIA faces antitrust scrutiny in the EU, export restrictions on China sales, and ongoing debate about AI's societal impact. The China H200 question remains unresolved as of May 2026 after Jensen's Trump summit visit.
